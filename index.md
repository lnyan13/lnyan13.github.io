I'm a social psychiatric epidemiologist by training, currently working as a Postdoctoral Fellow at the [University of Kansas Medical Center](https://www.kumc.edu) and as a Visiting Researcher at [Children's Mercy Kansas City](https://www.childrensmercy.org). I study the ways in which social, economic, and political factors affect community well-being. I often focus on mental health, but investigate broader concepts related to flourishing, life satisfaction, and poverty as well.

I'm especially interested in understanding how migration policies affect the composition of our communities and people's experience within those communities. I consider policies that directly legislate migration as well as those that may have an outsized impact on immigrants' lives. A major goal of my work is to understand these policies' dynamic consequences—not just for immigrants, but also for "hosting" communities at large.

I've collaborated with [World Vision](https://www.worldvision.org) to evaluate "Graduation model" livelihood programs among displaced communities in Rwanda, Zambia, and Somalia. Our team produced a [systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pstr.0000252) that identified major gaps in actionable evidence to support refugee livelihoods. In other recent projects I've also investigated the experience of statelessness from a legal-ethnographic perspective, analyzed humanitarian health systems to understand the viability of the ["humanitarian-development nexus,"](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0312788) and written [public commentary](https://sciencepolitics.org/2026/02/20/donors-can-invert-the-power-dynamics-of-global-mental-health-aid/) on how humanitarian donors can more strategically support local actors. My most recent research examines how social and economic conditions relate to mental health among Syrian refugees in Jordan.

In my early career, I spent over 10 years serving in direct practice and program leadership roles in domestic (USA) and international nonprofit organizations. I established and co-led the suicide prevention initiative for the Rohingya refugee response—one of the two dedicated working groups in the world addressing suicide in humanitarian settings—and contributed to the Inter-Agency Standing Committee's [global guidance document](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/addressing-suicide-in-humanitarian-settings--iasc-guidance) on this topic.

I received my doctoral training in the [Department of Mental Health](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/departments/mental-health) at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. I previously attended [Washington University in St. Louis](https://wustl.edu) for my undergraduate (psychology) program and completed my master's degree in social work at the [Brown School](https://brownschool.wustl.edu).
