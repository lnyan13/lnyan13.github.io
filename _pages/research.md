## Current projects

### Community health worker navigation under regulatory uncertainty: A mixed-methods study of women's preventive care linkage at Federally Qualified Health Centers in Wyandotte County

We are partnering with a local health center to investigate how recent policy changes may impact how (and whether) different groups of patients are accessing essential preventive care. We pair analyses of electronic medical records with provider and patient interviews to understand where patients are feeling the greatest impact and what clinics can do to best support them. We're also assessing how these changes affect the financial viability of clinics that rely heavily on Medicaid reimbursements. This study will identify and prioritize strategies for helping patients continue accessing care in a volatile policy climate.

- **Status:** Designing data extraction workflows; recruiting interview participants
- **Methods and data:** Explanatory sequential mixed methods design; interrupted time series with electronic medical record data followed by one-on-one interviews with clinic staff and patients
- **Team and funding:** With collaborators at a local health center (unnamed pending community advisory board review of the study dissemination plan); the University of Kansas Medical Center; and the University of Kansas. Funded by NCATS TL1TR002368.
- **Seeking:** Partners with access to comparable data from other settings; funding is currently available to support 1-2 research assistants (hourly)

### Allocating scarce perinatal outreach capacity in maternity care deserts: Development of transportable prediction models for rural primary care

We are developing a project to develop and test a tool that uses already-collected medical record data to identify patients at greatest risk of missing essential prenatal and postpartum care. This tool will support clinic staff like community health workers to reach patients and connect them to care in time to identify and address treatable complications. This tool can give rural clinics a practical way to direct limited staff time toward the patients who most need follow-up; it will also establish whether such tools remain accurate when used in different clinical contexts.

- **Status:** Preliminary planning
- **Methods and data:** Bayesian hierarchical modeling; internal-external cross-validation to assess model performance; pilot deployment with community health workers
- **Team and funding:** With collaborators from the Kansas Patients and Providers Engaged in Prevention Research (KPPEPR) Network, the University of Kansas Medical Center, and Children's Mercy Research Institute. Funding status pending grant review.
- **Seeking:** Clinic partners and community advisors; funding may become available to support up to 2 graduate students (up to 50% FTE each; timeline undetermined)

## Forthcoming projects

### Treaty Federalism as Collective Rights Framework: A Historical Legal Comparison of Self-Determination in Indigenous and Humanitarian Law

Ensuring the realization of human rights for over 123 million displaced people worldwide is one of our most pressing global justice issues. However, individualistic protections of international refugee law are limited; displaced people (especially those who are stateless) are reliably excluded from meaningfully participating in the decisions that shape their futures. This planned study will explore whether the legal logic of treaty federalism offers a viable framework for recognizing stateless communities as peoples with collective authority analogous to that exercised by Indigenous nations.

- **Planned start:** 2026-2028
- **Seeking:** Co-investigators and community partners with expertise in Indigenous legal frameworks, particularly related to Tribal sovereignty, and in international humanitarian law

### Documenting successful integration: Case studies of immigrant/refugee resettlement in rural, economically depressed regions

To inform effective and comprehensive immigration policies--especially for refugees, asylum-seekers, and others who have experienced extreme hardship--it is critical to understand what approaches to resettlement and integration have worked well, and why. This study will aim to systematically document the social, economic, and political features of several recent and ongoing immigration processes, focusing on rural and economically depressed areas that rely on immigration to sustain or grow the local workforce.

- **Planned start:** 2028-2030
- **Seeking:** Community partners, co-investigators for funding applications
